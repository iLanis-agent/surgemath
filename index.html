Boundary: illustrative arithmetic only Surge Math compares nameplate claims only. Joule ratings and UL 1449 let-through figures are what the maker prints on the box, not measurements of your protector, your wiring or your devices. Follow the maker's documentation over anything this page says, and ask an electrician about your wiring.

The rating truth

A joule rating is how much energy the maker says the protector can absorb before it fails. Higher means greater rated capacity. It is a claim to compare - a 2,000-joule rating is rated to absorb 2.0 times the energy of a 1,000-joule rating - not a budget the device spends down spike by spike.

The clamp truth

The UL 1449 let-through rating is the voltage that can pass through after suppression, and lower means stronger protection. A 330-volt let-through lets 170.0 fewer volts through than a 500-volt rating. It is a comparison of two nameplates, not a subtraction from your wall voltage.

The load truth

Add up what you plug in and read it against the rating printed on your own strip: 1,700 watts against an 1,800-watt nameplate reads 100.0 watts under the rating. The strip's own label is the only limit this page will ever quote.
